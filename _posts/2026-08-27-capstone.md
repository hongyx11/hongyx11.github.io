The capstone is a small, reproducible performance study. The objective is not to claim a breakthrough. It is to show that you can turn a broad performance question into defensible evidence.

## Choose one question

Examples:

- How does CSR SpMV performance change with row-length imbalance?
- When does OpenMP scheduling reduce imbalance enough to justify its overhead?
- How close does a streaming kernel come to measured memory bandwidth?
- Which input property best predicts runtime for a sparse kernel?
- How do compiler optimization flags change generated code and performance?

Keep the scope small enough to answer in two weeks.

## Required artifacts

Your repository should contain:

```text
capstone/
├── README.md
├── CMakeLists.txt
├── data/README.md
├── environment.md
├── results/raw/
├── scripts/
├── src/
└── report.md
```

Do not commit large matrix data. Document how to obtain it and verify it with a checksum.

## Evaluation checklist

### Correctness

- Is there a reference implementation or trusted comparison?
- Are tolerances stated?
- Are edge cases tested?

### Experimental method

- Can another student reproduce the environment and command?
- Are warm-up and repetitions documented?
- Are input, thread placement, and scheduler resources controlled?

### Analysis

- Is the limiting resource identified with a simple model?
- Does profiler evidence support the explanation?
- Are negative or unexpected results reported?

### Communication

- Do plots have units, legends, and useful captions?
- Does the report distinguish observation from interpretation?
- Are limitations and next steps stated?

## A five-part report

1. **Question and motivation** — one paragraph.
2. **System and method** — enough detail to reproduce the experiment.
3. **Results** — tables or plots including variability.
4. **Interpretation** — connect results to architecture and algorithm.
5. **Limitations and next experiment** — explain what remains unknown.

## When contacting me

Send a link to the repository and summarize the most surprising result in two or three sentences. A careful result that contradicts your initial hypothesis is more interesting than an unsupported speedup claim.
